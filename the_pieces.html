The Pawn

Pawns are the foot soldiers of chess. They move forward one square but capture diagonally. On their first move, they can move forward two squares. Pawns can be promoted to any other piece upon reaching the opposite end of the board.

The Rook

Rooks move vertically or horizontally any number of squares. They are powerful pieces, especially in the endgame, and work well together to control files and ranks.

The Knight

Knights move in an L-shape: two squares in one direction and then one square perpendicular. They are unique in their ability to jump over other pieces, making them excellent for creating forks and controlling the board.

The Bishop

Bishops move diagonally any number of squares. Each bishop starts on either a light or dark square and remains on that color throughout the game. Bishops are valuable for long-range attacks and working with other pieces for tactical combinations.

The Queen

The queen, the game's most powerful piece, moves vertically, horizontally, or diagonally. It combines the power of both a rook and a bishop, often leading attacks, creating threats, and supporting other pieces. The queen is essential for checkmating the opponent's king and achieving strategic dominance.

The King

The king moves one square in any direction. Although limited in mobility, it must be protected at all costs. The game's objective is to checkmate the opponent's king, putting it in a position where it cannot escape capture. The king is crucial for strategic planning, especially in the endgame, where it actively participates in creating checkmate patterns.
