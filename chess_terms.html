Capture

The act of taking an opponent's piece by moving one of your pieces to its square. This removes the captured piece from the board, gaining a material advantage or achieving a tactical goal, and is a fundamental aspect of chess play.

Check

A move that attacks the opponent's king, requiring an immediate response to remove the threat. The king must move out of check, block the check, or capture the attacking piece, emphasizing the constant vigilance needed to protect the king.

Promotion

When a pawn reaches the opponent's back rank and is promoted to a queen, rook, bishop, or knight. This significantly increases its value, altering the game's balance by adding a powerful piece that can greatly impact the game's outcome.

Draw

A game outcome where neither player wins. This can occur through stalemate, threefold repetition, fifty-move rule, mutual agreement, or insufficient mating material, resulting in equal points awarded to both players and reflecting balanced play.

Blunder

A significant mistake resulting in a severe loss of material or a greatly weakened position. This often changes the game's outcome from a winning or drawing position to a losing one, highlighting the importance of careful calculation and concentration.

Hanging

A piece that is undefended and can be captured without compensation. Identifying and attacking hanging pieces is a common tactical motif to gain a material advantage, often shifting the balance of the game significantly.

Checkmate

A position where the opponent's king is in check and has no legal move to escape, ending the game in victory for the player delivering the checkmate. This decisive move concludes the game and showcases successful strategy and tactics.

Fork

A single move that simultaneously attacks two or more of the opponent's pieces. This forces a significant material gain since the opponent can typically only move one piece to safety, making it a powerful tactical weapon.

Pin

A situation where a piece cannot move without exposing a more valuable piece behind it to capture. This creates a binding effect that restricts the pinned piece's movement and potentially leads to a material gain.

Skewer

A tactic where a more valuable piece is attacked first, and when it moves, a less valuable piece behind it is captured. This often leads to a significant material advantage, disrupting the opponent's strategy (Also Known As An X-Ray Attack)

Battery

A lineup of two or more pieces on the same file, rank, or diagonal working together to attack or control squares. This often involves a queen and rook or queen and bishop, creating powerful threats and increasing attacking potential.

Sacrifice

Voluntarily giving up material, usually a piece or pawn, to gain a tactical or strategic advantage. This aims for a stronger position, attacking chances, or achieving checkmate, demonstrating deep calculation and bold play.

Gambit

An opening strategy involving the deliberate sacrifice of a pawn or piece to gain a lead in development, open lines for the attack, or create dynamic imbalances favouring the sacrificing player. It introduces complexity and excitement early in the game.

Desperado

A piece sacrifices itself dramatically, capturing valuable material or giving perpetual check before being captured. This tactic often turns a losing position into a more advantageous or drawn one, creating unexpected opportunities and disrupting the opponent's strategy.

Zugzwang

A position where any move a player makes will worsen their situation, forcing them into a disadvantageous or losing position because they must move despite having no good options. This showcases the strategic depth of chess.
